C

The site, page by page

Small footprint on purpose. A café site fails by having twelve pages nobody maintains, not by having five that stay current.

Homecore
One strong photo, the line that says what Restless is, and — above the fold on a phone — hours, address, call, WhatsApp, directions. A live "open now / opens at 9" indicator computed from the hours data. Below that: three signature items, a short strip of the room, and the map.
Menucore
The page that earns the whole project. Sections that jump-link (Coffee, Breakfast, Pizza, Pasta, Bakery, Cold Drinks), photos on the items that deserve them, prices in EGP, and tags for vegetarian and spicy. Built from one data file so a price change is a one-line edit, never a redesign.
Cakes & Cateringcore
Where a bakery makes real money. Whole cakes, gateaux, party platters and office catering, with a short enquiry form that composes a pre-filled WhatsApp message — occasion, date, size, flavour, phone — rather than sending an email nobody checks.
Visitcore
Full hours table, embedded map with an "open in Google Maps" button, parking and the Degla landmark that actually helps people find 232 St., all three phone lines, and the delivery-app links.
Our Storycore
Who started it, when, why Maadi, what's baked in-house each morning. Short — 250 words and three good photos beats a page of history.
Galleryphase two
Only worth a page once there's proper photography. Until then the photos live inside the pages above.
Private eventsphase two
Book the space for birthdays and work meetings. Add it if that's a business line you want to grow.
D

Decisions that matter here

Cairo-specific things that are easy to get wrong and expensive to retrofit.

WhatsApp is the contact form

Almost nobody in Egypt fills out a web form and waits for an email. Every call-to-action on the site should open WhatsApp with the message already written. Contact forms stay as a fallback, not the main path.

Build it phone-first, and build it light

Nearly all traffic will arrive from a link in the Instagram or Facebook bio, on mobile data. The site should be a static page that loads fast on a weak connection — compressed images, no heavy frameworks, no cookie banner theatre.

Arabic decides the architecture

If the site is bilingual, that's a real RTL layout and two copies of the menu data — not a translate widget. It roughly doubles the content work but the build handles it cleanly if we plan for it from the first commit. Deciding this late is what makes it painful.

Be findable

Schema.org markup for a restaurant (hours, address, menu, price range) so Google can show hours and directions directly in search. Alongside that: a claimed and filled-in Google Business Profile, and proper Open Graph images so a shared link renders as a card instead of a bare URL.

The menu has to be editable without me

Prices in Egypt move. If updating a price requires a developer, the site is stale within a season and the whole thing quietly dies. Whichever build option we pick below, someone at Restless must be able to change a price in under a minute.

E

How to build it

ApproachWho edits the menuRunning costTrade-off
Hand-built static site Me, via a JSON file Domain only, ~$12/yr Fastest to ship and the cheapest to run, but you depend on me for every price change.
Static site + small CMS Anyone, in a browser login Domain only, ~$12/yr A day or two more work up front. The staff edit menu, hours and photos themselves, forever. Hosting stays free on Cloudflare Pages or Netlify.
WordPress + a café theme Anyone, in wp-admin ~$60–150/yr hosting Familiar, but slow on mobile, needs security updates, and the theme will fight you on Arabic. Not what I'd choose for a five-page site.

The middle row is my recommendation: a static site with a lightweight editor bolted on. It gives the speed of the first option and the independence of the third, and the hosting genuinely costs nothing at this traffic level — the only recurring bill is the domain.

G

Open questions

  • English, Arabic, or both?The one answer that changes the build rather than the content. Worth settling first.
  • Do prices go on the site?Some Cairo cafés keep them off to avoid reprinting. I'd publish them — it's the single most searched-for thing.
  • Should the site take orders itself, or hand off to the delivery apps?Hand off, for now. Building checkout and payments is a different project; linking to Talabat and elmenus costs nothing and works today.
  • Is there more than one branch, or plans for one?Changes how the Visit page and the address markup are structured.
  • Who at Restless will own the site after launch?A site with no owner goes stale in a season. Name the person before we launch, not after.
